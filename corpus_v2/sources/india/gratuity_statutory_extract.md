## Certified scope

This extract covers ordinary and fixed-term employee eligibility, the base
calculation, and the post-commencement wage basis. It deliberately does not
state a monetary ceiling because a current post-Code notification carrying the
ceiling has not yet been captured and certified in this repository.

For an ordinary employee, gratuity becomes payable after at least five years
of continuous service when employment ends through superannuation, retirement
or resignation. The five-year condition does not apply where termination is
caused by death or disablement. Section 53 also separately addresses expiry of
fixed-term employment; that exception must not be rewritten as a general
four-year eligibility threshold.

For every completed year of service, or a part exceeding six months, gratuity
is based on fifteen days of the employee's last-drawn wages. For a monthly
rated employee, fifteen days' wages is calculated by dividing the last-drawn
monthly wage by twenty-six and multiplying the result by fifteen. Special rules
for piece-rated and seasonal employees are outside this demo slice.

A directly engaged fixed-term employee is eligible for gratuity after rendering
service under the contract for one year. Gratuity for fixed-term employment is
paid on a pro-rata basis. This rule is specific to fixed-term employment and
does not reduce the ordinary employee threshold from five years.

The Labour Codes and their revised definition of wages apply to gratuity from
21 November 2025. For gratuity becoming payable on or after that date, the
Ministry FAQ states that the employee is paid using the last-drawn wage at the
time of the qualifying termination under the Code on Social Security, 2020;
the calculation is not split into pre-Code and post-Code service segments.
