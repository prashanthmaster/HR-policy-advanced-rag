# India — Statutory HR Law (Tier 1: real public statutory sources)

This file covers a bounded slice of Indian labour statute relevant to the case questions this project answers: gratuity, probation/notice, and leave. It is NOT a comprehensive restatement of Indian labour law.

Primary sources: Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 (as amended by the Payment of Gratuity (Amendment) Act, 2018); Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946 (probation/notice conventions); Factories Act, 1948 (annual leave, as commonly referenced pending full state-wise notification of the 2020 Labour Codes).

Each clause below is a separate retrieval chunk, delimited by a metadata block.

---
clause_id: IN-GRAT-S4-ELIG
country: India
doc_type: law
source_act: Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972
section: Section 4(1)
effective_date: 1972-09-16
version: current
source_url: https://clc.gov.in/clc/sites/default/files/PaymentofGratuityAct.pdf
---
Gratuity shall be payable to an employee on the termination of his employment after he has rendered continuous service for not less than five years — (a) on his superannuation, or (b) on his retirement or resignation, or (c) on his death or disablement due to accident or disease. Provided that the completion of continuous service of five years shall not be necessary where the termination of the employment of any employee is due to death or disablement.

clause_id: IN-GRAT-S4-FORMULA
country: India

source_act: Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972
section: Section 4(2)
effective_date: 1972-09-16
version: current
source_url: https://ssrana.in/articles/deciphering-the-payment-of-gratuity/
---
For every completed year of service or part thereof in excess of six months, the employer shall pay gratuity to an employee at the rate of fifteen days' wages (based on the rate of wages last drawn, comprising basic wage and dearness allowance) for every completed year of service, calculated as: (last drawn monthly wage / 26) × 15 × number of completed years of service.

clause_id: IN-STANDING-PROBATION
country: India
doc_type: law
source_act: Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946 — Model Standing Orders, common state adoption
section: Model Standing Order 4-A (probationer)
effective_date: 1946-04-23
version: current
source_url: https://labour.gov.in/
---
A "probationer" is a workman who is provisionally employed to fill a permanent vacancy and has not completed the probation period specified in the standing orders applicable to him. Where the standing orders do not specify a probation period, it shall be deemed to be three months, extendable up to a further period not exceeding three months at the discretion of the employer, on grounds of the workman's performance during the initial probation.

clause_id: IN-STANDING-NOTICE
country: India
doc_type: law
source_act: Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946 — Model Standing Orders, common state adoption
section: Model Standing Order 13
effective_date: 1946-04-23
version: current
source_url: https://labour.gov.in/
---
For a permanent workman, notice of termination shall be one month in writing on either side, or wages in lieu of notice for the period of the notice actually given, unless a longer notice period is specified in the workman's letter of appointment. No notice is required to terminate the services of a probationer or a temporary workman.

clause_id: IN-FACTORIES-LEAVE
country: India
doc_type: law
source_act: Factories Act, 1948
section: Section 79 (Annual leave with wages)
effective_date: 1948-09-23
version: current
source_url: https://labour.gov.in/
---
Every worker who has worked for a period of 240 days or more in a factory during a calendar year shall be allowed leave with wages in the subsequent calendar year at the rate of one day for every twenty days of work performed in the previous calendar year (approximately 15 days per year for a full year of qualifying work).

---
NOTE ON THE 2020 LABOUR CODES: India's four Labour Codes (Wage Code 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020) were enacted but, as of this corpus's assembly date, had not been brought into uniform force across all states via the required state-level rules notifications — several provisions (including a proposed uniform national definition of "wages" affecting gratuity/PF base pay, and a proposed 1-year continuous-service threshold for fixed-term employee gratuity) remain a live, evolving amendment surface. This corpus intentionally treats the pre-Codes statutes above (Gratuity Act 1972, Standing Orders Act 1946, Factories Act 1948) as the current-law baseline, and is where a genuine future freshness/versioning event would land once state notifications complete — do not assert Labour Code provisions as already in force without verifying against a specific state notification.
