# UAE — Statutory HR Law (Tier 1: real public statutory sources)

Primary source: Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 on the Regulation of Labour Relations (as amended), the UAE's private-sector labour law, in force since 2 February 2022, superseding Federal Law No. 8 of 1980.

The employer may appoint the worker under a probation period not exceeding six (6) months from the date of commencing work. The employer may terminate the worker's service during this period by notifying the worker in writing at least fourteen (14) days prior to the date specified for termination of service.

A worker may not be placed under probation more than once with the same employer. Upon successful completion of the probation period, the contract becomes valid under its agreed terms, and the probation period is counted as part of the worker's total period of service (relevant to gratuity and leave accrual).

Either party to the employment contract may terminate the contract for a legitimate reason, provided the other party is notified in writing and the notified party continues working during the notice period agreed in the contract, which shall be not less than thirty (30) days and not more than ninety (90) days.

A full-time foreign worker who has completed one year or more of continuous service is entitled to an end-of-service gratuity calculated on the basic wage, at the rate of: twenty-one (21) days' wage for each year of the first five years of service; and thirty (30) days' wage for each additional year beyond the first five years.

A worker is entitled to annual leave with full pay of not less than thirty (30) calendar days for each year of service exceeding one full year. A worker with a service period of more than six months but less than one year is entitled to leave calculated at two (2) days per month of service.
